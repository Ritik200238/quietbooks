# Self-audit, 2026-10-16

A first-party review of `contract/src/quietbooks.compact` (1,271 lines, 12 exported entry points)
and the audit-envelope validator in `contract/src/audit.ts`.

**This is not an independent audit.** It was written by the same party that wrote the contract, so
it carries the weakness that implies: a reviewer who misunderstood something when writing the code
will misunderstand it again when reviewing it. It is published because a reader is better served by
a list of known problems than by silence, and because every finding below can be checked against the
line it cites.

Nothing here has been fixed yet. Status is stated per finding.

| ID | Severity | Summary | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| QB-M-1 | Medium | Public timestamps are caller-asserted and unbounded | Open |
| QB-M-2 | Medium | Seller reliability counters can be manufactured | Open |
| QB-L-1 | Low | Contract declares a section for a circuit that does not exist | Open |

Two boundaries that are *not* findings — they are documented trade-offs — are recorded at the end,
because a reader who discovers them unaided would reasonably assume they had been missed.

## QB-M-1 — Public timestamps are caller-asserted and unbounded

**Severity:** Medium. No money and no trust-bearing counter depends on these values; every date a
reader sees does.

Six timestamps are written to public ledger state directly from a caller argument, with no
comparison to the chain's clock:

| Field | Circuit | Line |
|---|---|---|
| `issuedAt` | `issueInvoice` | `contract/src/quietbooks.compact:624` |
| `settledAt` | `settleWithNote` | `contract/src/quietbooks.compact:738` |
| `settledAt` | `settleAttested` | `contract/src/quietbooks.compact:801` |
| `releasedAt` | `releaseEscrow` | `contract/src/quietbooks.compact:948` |
| `resolvedAt` | `resolveDispute` | `contract/src/quietbooks.compact:1093` |
| `grantedAt` | `grantAudit` | `contract/src/quietbooks.compact:1179` |

The only constraint on any of them is relative: `assertTermsValid` requires
`frame.dueDate > issuedAt` (`contract/src/quietbooks.compact:521`), and both sides of that
comparison come from the same caller.

**What is not affected.** The forward-looking deadlines *are* bound to the chain clock —
`fundEscrow` asserts `blockTimeLt(deadline)` (line 925) and `grantAudit` asserts
`blockTimeLt(expiresAt)` (line 1194). The punctuality flag that feeds the reliability counters is
derived from the block, not from an argument: `onTimeNow` returns `!blockTimeGt(dueDate)`
(line 685). That was a deliberate fix and it holds.

**What is affected.** Every date on an invoice anchor and every date on a settlement record. A
seller can publish an invoice stamped with any year. A buyer can record a settlement dated before
the invoice was issued. Our own interface and the audit envelope present these as dates, which
invites a reader to treat them as facts about when something happened.

**Fix.** Pin each to a window around block time. The standard library has the comparisons needed —
`blockTimeGte` and `blockTimeGt`, documented at
`reference/midnight-docs/docs/compact/standard-library/exports.md:579` and `:587` — so for a
timestamp `t` and a tolerance `W`:

```compact
assert(blockTimeGte(t), "quietbooks: timestamp is in the future");
assert(!blockTimeGt(t + W), "quietbooks: timestamp is too far in the past");
```

A window is needed rather than equality because the caller builds the proof before the block that
contains it exists. Choosing `W` is the open question: too tight and an honest transaction that
waits in the mempool fails, too loose and the guarantee is thin.

## QB-M-2 — Seller reliability counters can be manufactured

**Severity:** Medium. The reliability record is the part of the product a counterparty is asked to
trust, and it can be fabricated by one party acting alone at the cost of transaction fees.

`settleAttested` exists so an invoice paid over a rail the chain cannot see — a bank transfer, a
payment made in an earlier block — can still be recorded. It deliberately requires no payment: the
comment at `contract/src/quietbooks.compact:793` argues the seller is the party who loses by marking
an unpaid invoice settled, so the incentive points the right way.

That argument holds for a seller who is lying to a real buyer. It does not hold for a seller with
no buyer at all.

`issueInvoice` takes `buyerKey` as a plain `Bytes<32>` argument
(`contract/src/quietbooks.compact:621`) and stores it unexamined. The only check is that it differs
from the seller's key and is not zero (`assertTermsValid`, lines 523 and 525). Nothing requires it
to be a key anybody holds. So:

1. The seller calls `issueInvoice` with 32 arbitrary bytes as `buyerKey`.
2. The seller calls `settleAttested` on it, which needs only their own key and their own terms.
3. `bumpSettled(anchor.sellerKey, onTime)` runs (line 827), incrementing `settled` and — if the
   due date they chose has not passed — `settledOnTime`.

Repeat. There is no rate limit; `issuedCount.increment(1)` is the only other effect.

**Why the obvious patch does not work.** Requiring the buyer to co-sign would make `settleAttested`
useless for the case it exists for. Requiring `buyerKey` to be a key with history just moves the
problem: a party can derive unlimited keys from one secret, because `callerKey(pin)` is
`hash(tag, salt, pin, secret)` (line 412) and the PIN is theirs to choose.

**Fix — a design decision, not a patch.** The honest options:

- Count only settlements that moved shielded value, so `settleWithNote` and the escrow release path
  feed the counters and `settleAttested` does not. This makes the number mean less but makes it
  true.
- Keep one counter per settlement mode and publish the breakdown, so a reader can discount attested
  settlements themselves. `Reliability` currently records `settled`, `settledOnTime`, `cancelled`,
  `disputesOpened` and `disputesLost` with no mode split.

The second is preferred: it does not throw away information, and it puts the judgement where it
belongs. Not yet implemented.

## QB-L-1 — A section header with nothing under it

**Severity:** Low. Documentation accuracy.

`contract/src/quietbooks.compact:1235` opens a section titled "Reliability proof". No circuit
follows it; the next code is the administration section. The README already discloses that the
circuit is not built (`README.md:393`, under "What is not built yet"), so the repository does not
contradict itself — but a reader working through the contract alone is told a feature is there and
then is not.

Related: `e2e/src/run.ts:11` describes a step in the end-to-end flow as "prove reliability". That
step reads the counters back through the indexer; it proves nothing.

**Fix.** Delete the header or mark it pending, and reword the e2e comment to say what the step does.

## Boundaries, not findings

These two are already stated in the repository. They are repeated here because a reader who finds
them unaided would reasonably assume they had been missed.

**Escrow publishes the amount.** `fundEscrow` calls `escrowVault.insertCoin`
(`contract/src/quietbooks.compact:938`), which puts the coin in the contract's custody, and a
contract that holds a coin publishes what it holds. The private settlement path avoids this by
forwarding the coin in the same call so the balance change is zero; escrow cannot, because the whole
point is to hold. `README.md:49` states it and the interface says so at the point of choosing.

**The envelope's expiry check is advisory.** `validateAuditEnvelope` takes `now` as an argument
(`contract/src/audit.ts:1067`) and the expiry check compares against it (line 1113). An auditor
validating in their own browser is checking against their own clock. The authoritative `expiresAt`
is the one on the chain's grant; the local check exists so an auditor does not accidentally rely on
a lapsed grant, and an auditor who feeds it a false `now` only deceives themselves. Seven of the
eight checks are cryptographic and do not depend on it.

## What this review did not cover

- The TypeScript interface, CLI and UI beyond `contract/src/audit.ts`.
- Proof-system-level questions. Whether the compiled circuits enforce what the source says is taken
  on the compiler's word.
- Economic analysis of the escrow and dispute incentives beyond the specific case in QB-M-2.
- Anything on a public network, because nothing has been deployed to one yet.
